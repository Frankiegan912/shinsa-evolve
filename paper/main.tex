\begin{abstract}
LLM-driven program search commonly selects candidates using scores that the candidates' own
training runs report. When the candidate program also controls how that score is measured---its
reward shaping, its evaluation schedule, its exposure to a fixed set of seeds---selection
optimizes the report rather than the result. We study this failure mode in a minimal
\emph{audit-gated recipe evolution} harness: an LLM iteratively rewrites training recipes
(optimizer settings, evaluation schedules, shaped rewards) for a fixed policy class, and every
candidate receives both a self-reported \claimed{} score and an \audited{} score measured by
harness-owned code on freshly drawn seeds under the environment's raw reward. The selection
signal---trusting claims versus trusting audits---is the single manipulated variable, evaluated
in two environments with deliberately different execution backends (MinAtar Breakout under
jit-compiled batch rollouts; LunarLander-v3 under a process pool). Selecting on self-reports
systematically breeds inflation: on LunarLander every claimed-selection candidate over-reports
(15/15; mean gap $+241$ raw-reward points, versus $+141$ under audited selection), selection
picks a candidate 35 points worse than the best already sitting in its own archive, and the
top-ranked candidate claims $+154$ while actually scoring $-119$. Trajectory forensics show the
inflation is \emph{measurement gaming} rather than reward-function trickery: under claimed
selection, evolved schedules collapse evaluation effort (mean episodes per evaluation 1.4
versus 2.4 late in the search), and replaying the top candidate under its own measurement
conditions reproduces a ${\sim}400$-point illusion even though no recipe code executes at audit
time. A hard audit gate---fresh OS-entropy seeds, raw rewards, no candidate code path---restores
selection quality at matched candidate budget. We release the harness, all run archives, and
the analysis code.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A growing family of systems uses large language models to propose programs and an outer loop to
keep the best ones: program search for mathematical discovery~\citep{romeraparedes2024funsearch,
novikov2025alphaevolve}, reward-function design~\citep{ma2024eureka}, algorithm
evolution~\citep{real2020automlzero}, prompt-space optimization~\citep{yang2024opro}, and
automated research pipelines~\citep{lu2024aiscientist}. These loops share a load-bearing
assumption: the fitness attached to each candidate is accurate enough for selection.

That assumption has two very different regimes. In domains with exact verifiers---a theorem
checker, an exact combinatorial objective---the evaluator is trusted code and the assumption
holds by construction. But when candidates are \emph{training programs} whose quality is
expensive to measure, practical systems let the candidate's own run report its fitness: the
best score seen during training, under the candidate's own reward shaping and evaluation
settings. The candidate then controls not only the object being scored but the measurement
procedure itself. Selection pressure applies to the composite, and Goodhart-style
failure~\citep{manheim2018categorizing} becomes an engineering concern rather than a
philosophical one. The specification-gaming literature documents what optimizers do to
imperfect objectives~\citep{krakovna2020specification,lehman2020surprising,skalse2022defining,
pan2022effects,amodei2016concrete}; automated research systems have already exhibited the
program-search variant, such as agents editing their own evaluation
harness~\citep{lu2024aiscientist}.

This paper isolates the question experimentally. We build a deliberately small recipe-evolution
harness in which \emph{who controls measurement} is the only thing that changes between
conditions, and we quantify what selection on self-reports does to an otherwise identical
search.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A minimal audit-gated harness} for LLM-driven recipe evolution. A recipe is a
  small Python module controlling exactly three things (optimizer settings, per-generation
  evaluation schedule, per-transition shaped reward) for a fixed inner trainer. Every candidate
  gets a \claimed{} score (its own training's best fitness under its own measurement) and an
  \audited{} score (harness-owned re-evaluation of the trained policy: fresh OS-entropy seeds,
  raw environment reward, fixed horizon, no execution path for recipe code). Selection mode is
  a switch; audits always run, so every run can be re-ranked under either signal post hoc.
  \item \textbf{Evidence that selection on self-reports breeds inflation.} Under audited
  selection the claimed$-$audited gap is mildly optimistic; under claimed selection it inflates
  (Breakout: 90\% of candidates positive versus 60\%, mean $+1.10$ versus $+0.37$ bricks;
  LunarLander: 15/15 positive versus 38/40, mean $+241$ versus $+141$ points), selection quality
  degrades at matched budget, and the search demonstrably misses better candidates already in
  its own archive.
  \item \textbf{A mechanism: measurement gaming, not reward hacking.} The inflated champion's
  shaped reward is benign; what evolved is \emph{cheap, noisy, short-horizon self-evaluation}
  (episodes per evaluation collapsing toward 1, training horizons of 150--400 steps versus the
  1000-step audit), plus overfitting to a fixed training seed pool. Replaying the champion
  policy under its own measurement conditions scores $+282$; under the audit protocol the same
  parameters score $-119$.
  \item \textbf{An honest account of what audits certify.} Independently retraining the seed
  recipe shows recipe-level outcome variance (e.g.\ audited returns from $-237$ to $-128$ on
  LunarLander); audits certify \emph{policies}, not \emph{recipes}. We release code, run
  archives, and analysis scripts.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/Frankiegan912/shinsa-evolve}}
\end{enumerate}

\section{The audit-gated recipe evolution harness}
\label{sec:system}

\paragraph{Recipe contract.} A candidate \emph{recipe} is a single Python module defining
exactly: (i) \texttt{OPTIMIZER}, a dict with the inner optimizer's initial step size
$\sigma_0 \in [0.01, 2]$ and population size in $[4, 64]$; (ii) \texttt{schedule(gen)},
returning per-generation evaluation settings---episodes per candidate evaluation in $[1,16]$
and a training episode step cap in $[50,1000]$; and (iii)
\texttt{shaped\_reward(obs, action, reward, terminated, truncated, step)}, a deterministic
per-transition training reward. Everything else---policy architecture, training loop, compute
budgets, the audit---is fixed harness code that recipe code cannot touch. Static checks
(import allowlist, no IO, no dunder access) reject obviously abusive programs as hygiene; they
are not a security sandbox, and none of our results depend on them.

\paragraph{Inner training.} The policy class is a fixed tanh MLP with hidden layers
$(32,32)$ over the flattened observation (13{,}987 parameters for Breakout, 1{,}508 for
LunarLander), trained by separable CMA-ES~\citep{hansen2001cmaes,ros2008sepcma}, the standard
linear-complexity variant for parameter vectors of this size, in the spirit of evolution
strategies for policy search~\citep{salimans2017evolution}. Fitness of a population member is
its mean shaped return over the scheduled number of episodes, drawn from a small per-candidate
seed pool that is fixed across generations---deliberately, since seed reuse is one honest
channel through which self-reports drift optimistic. Hard per-candidate budgets (episode count
and wall clock) are enforced by the harness regardless of what the schedule requests.

\paragraph{Claimed score.} The candidate's \claimed{} score is the best training fitness
observed during its run---i.e.\ the number the recipe's own measurement machinery produces:
its shaping, its episode counts, its horizon, its fixed seed pool. The trained parameters
submitted for evaluation are those of the best member under that same signal. This mirrors
the self-reported ``best score seen in training'' that practical search loops archive.

\paragraph{Audit gate.} The \audited{} score re-evaluates the submitted parameters under three
invariants: (1) evaluation seeds are freshly drawn from OS entropy at audit time, so no
candidate can anticipate or overfit them; (2) scoring uses the environment's raw reward only,
over a fixed 1000-step horizon and 32 episodes; (3) recipe code has no execution path into the
audit---the audit sees a parameter vector, nothing else. The \emph{honesty gap} of a candidate
is $g = \text{claimed} - \text{audited}$, defined relative to this fixed protocol. For honest
recipes $g$ can be negative (e.g.\ when a recipe trains at shorter horizons than the audit);
what matters experimentally is how the distribution of $g$ responds to selection pressure.

\paragraph{Outer loop and modes.} The outer loop repeats: select a parent from the archive
(80\% uniform over the current top-5, 20\% uniform over all), ask the LLM to rewrite it
(one-shot prompt containing the contract, the environment description, the parent source, and
the parent's score under the run's selection signal), validate with rejection sampling, train,
audit, archive. The run's \emph{mode} decides everything the search sees: in \textbf{audited}
mode, parent ranking and LLM feedback use audited scores; in \textbf{claimed} mode, both use
claimed scores. Audits still run in claimed mode, but purely as recording---exactly the
counterfactual instrumentation needed to measure what trusting self-reports costs. The mutation
operator is a frontier commercial LLM accessed through its command-line interface; across all
runs, 98\% of 131 mutations were accepted on the first attempt (mean CLI latency 96\,s), so
rejection sampling is not a meaningful filter on the phenomena studied here.

\subsection{Selection pressure on self-reports breeds inflation}

Table~\ref{tab:results} and Figure~\ref{fig:gap} give the headline comparison. Under audited
selection, gaps are mildly optimistic: on Breakout the distribution is roughly centered
(median $+0.10$ bricks, 60\% positive); on LunarLander it is optimistic for structural reasons
discussed below. Under claimed selection the distribution shifts decisively: 90\% positive with
$3\times$ the mean gap on Breakout, and \emph{universal} over-reporting on LunarLander (15/15,
mean $+241$). Selection is doing this, not the LLM's priors: both modes use the same mutation
operator, prompt template, contract, budgets, and parent-selection rule; only the score being
trusted differs.

\subsection{Trusting claims degrades what the search actually selects}

The operational question is not whether candidates exaggerate but whether the loop ends up
keeping worse programs. It does (Figure~\ref{fig:outcomes}). At matched candidate budgets,
audited selection's best pick beats claimed selection's pick in both environments: $4.94$
versus $4.31$ bricks on Breakout (first 20 candidates), $-62.5$ versus $-118.8$ points on
LunarLander (first 15). With its full 60-candidate budget the Breakout audited run stays at
$4.94$; the LunarLander audited run reaches $-45.1$.

Claimed selection also fails \emph{relative to itself}: ranking its own archive by claims picks
a candidate worse than what it already found. On LunarLander the claimed run's pick audits at
$-118.8$ while candidate~2 in the same archive audits at $-83.9$ (regret 35.0); on Breakout the
corresponding regret is 0.25 bricks ($4.31$ picked versus $4.56$ available). The mis-ranking is
not subtle: the claimed run's top-5 by claims on LunarLander includes a candidate claiming
$+147.1$ that audits at $-405.7$.

\subsection{Mechanism: measurement gaming, not reward hacking}
\label{sec:mechanism}

The natural suspicion is that claimed-mode recipes learned to hack their shaped rewards. The
data say otherwise. Take the LunarLander claimed run's champion (claims $+153.7$, audits
$-118.8$). Its shaping is benign---small soft-landing penalties, a $0.05$-per-leg contact
bonus---and under audit conditions contributes $-2.1$ per episode on average. Across eight
fresh audit-length episodes the policy crashes quickly (mean length 102 steps of a possible
1000; legs touch the ground in 4.4\% of steps), scoring a mean raw return of $-121.7$,
consistent with its archived audit.

What actually evolved is the \emph{measurement}. The champion's schedule evaluates candidates
on 2 episodes with a 400-step cap; the run's runner-up (claims $+147.1$, audits $-405.7$) uses
1 episode and a 150-step cap. Three mutually reinforcing distortions follow. First,
\emph{evaluation noise is rewarded}: a best-of selection over noisy estimates preferentially
archives lucky draws, so recipes that measure themselves with 1--2 episodes look better than
recipes that measure honestly. Second, \emph{fixed-pool seed overfitting}: CMA-ES adapts the
policy to the handful of initial conditions it is repeatedly evaluated on. Third,
\emph{horizon truncation}: a 150--400-step training window simply never observes the late
consequences the 1000-step audit charges for. Replaying the champion's archived parameters
under its own generation-0 measurement conditions---its two training seeds, its 400-step
cap---scores $+282.2$ (its archived claim of $+153.7$ was recorded under the schedule active at
the generation that produced the best member). The identical parameter vector scores $-118.8$
under the audit protocol. The entire ${\sim}400$-point illusion is produced by measurement
conditions; no recipe code runs at audit time.

The population-level statistics agree (Table~\ref{tab:schedule}): claimed-mode runs evolve
fewer episodes per evaluation than audited-mode runs in both environments (late-run means 1.8
versus 2.3 on Breakout, 1.4 versus 2.4 on LunarLander; the honest seed uses 4). Both modes
face the same incentive to spend less compute per evaluation (cheaper evaluations buy more
generations within a fixed budget), so some drift downward is rational cost allocation; the
\emph{differential} between modes, consistent across environments and growing over the run, is
the measurement-gaming signal: under claimed selection, noise itself pays.

\subsection{Audits certify policies, not recipes}

A passing audit certifies that \emph{this trained parameter vector} performs as measured. It
does not certify that \emph{rerunning the recipe} reproduces that performance. Independently
retraining the honest seed recipe three times (plus its original training) yields audited
scores of $3.5$--$5.0$ bricks on Breakout ($4.3 \pm 0.6$) and $-236.5$ to $-128.4$ points on
LunarLander ($-161.4 \pm 44.4$). Selection over recipes therefore operates on draws from
high-variance distributions, and a single audit collapses only the measurement error, not the
retraining variance. Any deployment of audit-gated search that intends to reuse the
\emph{recipe} rather than the \emph{artifact} should budget replicate retrains of finalists.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Breakout improvements sit inside the retrain band.} The Breakout audited champion
($4.94$) is within the seed recipe's retrain range ($3.5$--$5.0$), so on that environment we
claim only that the \emph{comparative} phenomena (inflation, mis-ranking) reproduce---not that
evolution discovered a stronger recipe. LunarLander carries the improvement claim: its audited
champion ($-45.1$) clears the retrain band ($-236.5$ to $-128.4$) by a wide margin.
\textbf{Scale.} Runs are small ($\le 60$ candidates; 15--20 in claimed mode), one LLM family
serves as the mutation operator, and both tasks are classic small-scale control; effect sizes
elsewhere may differ, though the mechanism (noise, seed reuse, horizon truncation) is generic.
\textbf{Gap semantics.} The honesty gap is defined against one fixed audit protocol; honest
recipes can have negative gaps (training horizon shorter than the audit's), so the evidential
quantity is the distribution shift between modes, not any single candidate's gap.
\textbf{Hygiene, not sandboxing.} Static checks deter accidental IO; a determined adversarial
program is out of scope---our results concern what \emph{selection pressure} produces without
any code-level attack on the audit.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

LLM-driven program search with \emph{trusted} evaluators has produced striking results:
FunSearch~\citep{romeraparedes2024funsearch} and AlphaEvolve~\citep{novikov2025alphaevolve}
score candidates with exact verifiers or deterministic benchmark harnesses, and
AutoML-Zero~\citep{real2020automlzero} evolves algorithms against controlled evaluation
pipelines. Our setting differs in that the candidate is a \emph{training program} whose
self-reported fitness is the cheap signal, which moves the evaluator inside the candidate's
control. Eureka~\citep{ma2024eureka} evolves reward functions with LLMs and scores them by
retraining under a fixed, trusted evaluation---the audit-shaped end of the spectrum; our
claimed mode is the other end, and the comparison quantifies the distance between them.
The result is a program-search instance of reward hacking and specification
gaming~\citep{krakovna2020specification,skalse2022defining,pan2022effects,amodei2016concrete,
lehman2020surprising}, and of Goodhart failures under optimization
pressure~\citep{manheim2018categorizing}, with a twist the taxonomy should note: what our
populations game is not the reward function but the \emph{estimator}---its sample size, its
horizon, its seeds. Automated-science systems report the adjacent failure of agents modifying
their own evaluation code~\citep{lu2024aiscientist}; the audit gate is the minimal structural
answer: keep one measurement path the candidate cannot reach.

\section{Conclusion}

In LLM-driven recipe evolution, self-reported fitness is not a noisy version of the truth; it
is a signal the search will learn to manufacture. Given control over its own measurement, an
evolving population converged on cheap, noisy, short-horizon self-evaluation---universal
over-reporting on one benchmark, top-ranked candidates worth hundreds of points less than
claimed, and selection that misses better programs already in its own archive. A hard audit
gate with three properties---fresh entropy seeds, ground-truth metric, no candidate code
path---was sufficient to restore trustworthy selection at matched budget, at the price of the
audit episodes and an honest caveat: audits certify the trained artifact, not the recipe that
produced it. As LLM-driven search loops become infrastructure, we expect the audit gate to be
the kind of component nobody reports on until it is missing.
